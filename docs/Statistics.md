
# Statistical Analysis of Quantum Entropy

## 1. Overview

The goal of this analysis is to verify that the generated bitstream follows a **Discrete Uniform Distribution**. In an ideal Quantum Random Number Generator (QRNG), every byte value $x \in \{0, 1, \dots, 255\}$ should have an equal probability of occurring:

$$P(X = x) = \frac{1}{256} \approx 0.0039$$

## 2. Graphical Representation: The Histogram

The real-time histogram generated by `stats_analysis.py` allows us to visually inspect the entropy quality.

* **Uniformity:** In a healthy system, the heights of the 256 bins should be approximately equal, forming a "flat" distribution.
* **Bias Detection:** If the hardware (Zener diode or ADC) has an offset, certain bins will appear significantly higher than others. This visual cue often indicates that the **Von Neumann extractor** is needed to de-bias the source.
* **Entropy Collapse:** A single dominant peak indicates a hardware failure or a "stuck" bit, where the system repeats a specific value.

## 3. The Chi-Squared ($\chi^2$) Goodness-of-Fit Test

To move beyond visual inspection, we use the Pearson's Chi-squared test to mathematically determine if the observed frequencies deviate significantly from the expected uniform distribution.

### The Formula

The $\chi^2$ statistic is calculated as follows:

$$\chi^2 = \sum_{i=0}^{255} \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}$$

Where:

* $O_i$: The **Observed** frequency (number of times byte value $i$ appeared).
* $E_i$: The **Expected** frequency ($E_i = \frac{\text{Total Samples}}{256}$).

### Degrees of Freedom

For a distribution with $k$ possible outcomes, the degrees of freedom ($df$) is:

$$df = k - 1 = 256 - 1 = 255$$

## 4. Interpretation: P-Value and Significance

The test yields a **P-value**, which represents the probability of observing a  result at least as extreme as the one calculated, assuming the null hypothesis (the distribution is uniform) is true.

* **$P > 0.05$:** We **fail to reject** the null hypothesis. The data is statistically indistinguishable from a true random source.
* **$P < 0.05$:** We **reject** the null hypothesis. There is a statistically significant bias, suggesting the entropy source is compromised or influenced by external noise (EMI).

## 5. Statistical Moving Window

The analysis uses a **Moving Window** of 5,000 samples (default). This allows the system to:

1. Provide enough data points for the $\chi^2$ test to be valid (Rule of thumb: $E_i \ge 5$).
2. Detect "Drift" in the hardware over time, such as thermal changes in the Zener diode.

## 6. Autocorrelation Analysis ($R(\tau)$)

While the Chi-squared test verifies the distribution of values, it does not detect temporal patterns. For example, a sequence like `0, 1, 2, 3...` is perfectly uniform but completely predictable.

**Autocorrelation** measures the correlation between a sequence and a shifted (lagged) version of itself.

### The Mathematical Model

For a discrete sequence $x_i$ with mean $\mu$, the autocorrelation coefficient at lag $\tau$ is defined as:

$$R(\tau) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N-\tau} (x_i - \mu)(x_{i+\tau} - \mu)}{\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu)^2}$$

### Interpretations for QRNG

* $R(0) = 1$ **:** A sequence is always perfectly correlated with itself at zero shift.
* $R(\tau \neq 0) \approx 0$ **:** For a true quantum source, there should be no linear relationship between samples. In our plot, the correlation should stay within the **95% confidence interval** (marked by red dashed lines):

$$\text{Bounds} = \pm \frac{1.96}{\sqrt{N}}$$

* **Significant Peaks:** If a peak exceeds these bounds at a specific lag (e.g., $\tau=8$), it indicates a "leakage" of a periodic pattern, likely caused by power line interference (50/60Hz) or a flaw in the bit-packing logic.
